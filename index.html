About the RED Tool

The Researcher Embeddedness Statement (RES) is a comprehensive self-assessment tool designed to help researchers evaluate and articulate their positioning in relation to their research community and context.

Embeddedness refers to the degree to which a researcher has direct connections, experience, and relationships with the community, country, or group they are studying.

It encompasses your accumulated social capital, shared cultural and linguistic competence, duration and quality of relationships, prior research experience in the context, and ongoing engagement with institutional structures.

  • Research Validity: Understanding your positionality ensures more rigorous and transparent research
  • Ethical Research: Awareness of positioning enables ethical engagement with communities
  • Methodological Transparency: Explicitly discussing embeddedness strengthens methodology sections
  • Reflexivity: The tool encourages critical reflection on how your relationship influences interpretation
  • Community Respect: Demonstrating awareness of your positioning builds trust with research communities

The tool evaluates embeddedness across eight key dimensions:

  1. Country/Society Relationship: Your connection to the geographic and social context
  2. Community Connection: Direct relationships with the specific community being studied
  3. Language Proficiency: Your ability to communicate in the community's primary language(s)
  4. Research Experience: Prior research experience in this context
  5. Field Presence: Duration and intensity of physical engagement with the research site
  6. Community Collaboration: Involvement of community members in research design and implementation
  7. Positionality Awareness: Your reflexive understanding of how your positioning influences research
  8. Data & Methodological Constraints: How your positioning shaped what you could access and study

Highly Embedded: Strong connections and deep engagement with the research community. You demonstrate extensive knowledge, established relationships, and cultural competence. Requires active management of insider bias.

Moderately Embedded: Balanced insider and outsider positioning. You combine contextual knowledge with analytical distance, offering both community understanding and critical perspective.

Minimally Embedded: Limited but meaningful connections to the community. Your external positioning provides analytical advantages while requiring deliberate strategies for building cultural understanding.

Weakly Embedded: Primarily external positioning with minimal community relationships. Your outsider status enables critical distance while requiring substantial investment in relationship building and community engagement.

Your embeddedness profile is not a judgment of your research quality. Rather, it's a framework for understanding your positioning and strategically articulating how it shaped your research.

Each profile has distinct advantages: Highly embedded researchers bring insider knowledge; moderately embedded researchers bridge perspectives; minimally/weakly embedded researchers provide critical distance.

The key is to explicitly discuss your positioning in your methodology section and reflexively address how it influenced interpretation.

The RES Tool was created and authored by Dr Noha Atef, Assistant Professor in Journalism and Global Media at the University of Galway, Ireland.

Dr Atef specializes in digital media, citizen journalism, health communication, and media activism in the Arab world, with extensive fieldwork experience. Her research explores researcher positionality and reflexivity in cross-cultural and cross-national research contexts.
